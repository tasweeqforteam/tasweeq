# 08 · Poster & Advertising Design

A poster or ad is a communication machine with a narrow attention window.
Decide the following before composing:

```
Primary message (≤8 words) ·  Audience ·  Viewing distance & dwell time
Dominant focal point       ·  Required info (P2/P3) ·  Product/subject importance
Brand visibility level     ·  CTA priority           ·  Concept (one sentence)
```

## 1. Attention budget by placement

| Placement | Dwell | Copy budget | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feed thumb-stop | ~1–2 s | headline ≤7 words | one focal point; headline legible at phone size |
| Story / Reel | 2–5 s | headline + CTA | vertical stack, safe zones |
| Street poster / OOH | 3–7 s at distance | ≤7 words + brand | huge type, maximum value contrast |
| Event / cultural poster | 10 s+ up close | info block allowed | P1 image/type gesture plus a structured info zone |
| Print ad (magazine) | 3–10 s | headline + short body | editorial quality expected |

## 2. Concept engines (choose one; do not stack them)

- **Visual metaphor**: merge the product with an idea (a lemon split as a sunrise means freshness). It must read without the headline, and the headline then adds the twist.
- **Exaggeration / scale shift**: the benefit made literal and huge.
- **Juxtaposition**: before/after, problem/answer in one frame.
- **Type as image**: the headline *is* the visual (Arabic letterforms excel here; see REF-0008).
- **Omission / negative space**: show less and let the viewer close the shape.
- **Product as hero**: premium light, material truth, one idea of context.

Test: describe the ad in one sentence without adjectives. If the sentence is
"a product with text", there is no concept yet.

## 3. Composition levers

- **Scale contrast**: the P1 element is at least 2–3× the visual weight of P2.
- **Controlled cropping**: crop into the subject for intimacy and tension. Never crop at joints, through faces' eyes, or through the logo.
- **Compositional tension**: one deliberate near-touch, diagonal or bleed. The rest stays calm.
- **Strategic negative space**: the empty area points to the message or product.
- **Deliberate contrast**: value contrast drives legibility; colour contrast drives emotion. Check value first (squint test).
- **Brand integration**: a logo zone fixed across a campaign. Brand colour or device carries recognition before the logo is read.
- **CTA**: one action. It must be visually findable last (end of the eye path), not loudest.

## 4. Anti-patterns

Complexity as a substitute for an idea · three focal points · a headline over a
busy image area (QA contrast catches the pixels; you must catch the
composition) · fake urgency badges · stock clichés (handshake, lightbulb,
rocket) · text inside generated imagery · logo enlarged to "fill space".
